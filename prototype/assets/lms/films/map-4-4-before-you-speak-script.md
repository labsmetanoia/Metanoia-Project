# Before you speak: the three elements of presentation style

Film `map-4-4-before-you-speak` · The Map 4.4

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives and reading: the three elements of presentation style, grounding yourself before you speak, and adjusting to the room. These are standard practices of workplace communication, taught here in the lesson's own words. The case and the character are invented for this film and appear in no other course. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (125 s)

- `00:04` Tika knows her material. But she walks in looking at the floor, and her hands will not stay still.
- `00:13` The audience reads your body before it hears your words. If the two disagree, the body wins.
- `00:21` Presentation style has three elements.
- `00:24` Physical presence: what they see.
- `00:28` Vocal emphasis: what they hear under the words.
- `00:32` And emotional connection: what they feel when you read the room and respond.
- `00:39` Presence is prepared, not performed. Before she walks in next time, Tika does three things. Feet flat on the floor.
- `00:48` Three slow breaths, longer out than in.
- `00:52` And a home for her hands, so no part of her mind has to watch them.
- `00:59` All of it before she walks in, like an athlete warming up.
- `01:06` The same elements change with the room. One to one: stillness, eye contact, a slower pace.
- `01:13` A small meeting: sit forward, and use your voice to mark the answer.
- `01:20` A large room: wider gestures, a slower pace, real pauses.
- `01:25` A video call: the camera flattens you, so look at the lens and add energy.
- `01:32` The next presentation has the same material. This time her body says what her words say.
- `01:40` She did not copy anyone. She adjusted the elements, not her personality.
- `01:46` The body speaks first.
- `01:49` Presence is prepared, not performed.
- `01:52` Now it is your turn. Before your next meeting: feet, three breaths, a home for your hands.
