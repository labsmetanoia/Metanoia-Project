## English (84 s)

- `00:00` One sentence: setting a learning intention.
- `00:03` Every January, Dimas writes the same line and sticks it on his wall: this year, I will get better at data analysis.
- `00:09` By March, the note is still there. Nothing else has changed.
- `00:15` The problem is not his effort. It is the sentence. “Better” is an adjective. It gives him nothing to check.
- `00:22` So he crosses it out. The new sentence has a result he can count, and a date: by 30 June, three finished projects, shown to a mentor.
- `00:31` Next, time. His first plan is two hours every day. It survives nine days.
- `00:37` His second plan is smaller: three sessions of forty minutes, booked in his calendar like classes he cannot skip.
- `00:44` A modest plan you keep beats an ambitious one you imagine.
- `00:49` Last, a signal. Every Sunday evening, Dimas checks one number: sessions kept this week.
- `00:55` Three of three, carry on. One of three, make the plan smaller, not the guilt bigger.
- `01:03` Put together, a working intention has four parts: a result you can count, a date, a weekly time budget you can defend, and one signal you check every Sunday.
- `01:12` Intentions with a number and a date get finished. Intentions with adjectives get abandoned.
- `01:19` Now it is your turn. Write your one sentence for The Map: a result, a date, a weekly budget and a Sunday signal.
