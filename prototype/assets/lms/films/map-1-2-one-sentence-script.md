# One sentence: setting a learning intention

Film `map-1-2-one-sentence` · The Map 1.2

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives: a one-sentence intention with a result and a date, a weekly time budget that is defended, and one signal checked every Sunday. The story and the character are invented. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (126 s)

- `00:04` Every January, Dimas writes the same line and sticks it on his wall: this year, I will get better at data analysis.
- `00:13` By March, the note is still there. Nothing else has changed.
- `00:19` The problem is not his effort. It is the sentence.
- `00:24` “Better” is an adjective. It gives him nothing to check.
- `00:29` So he crosses it out.
- `00:33` The new sentence has a result he can count, and a date: by 30 June, three finished projects, shown to a mentor.
- `00:42` Next, time. His first plan is two hours every day. It survives nine days.
- `00:49` His second plan is smaller: three sessions of forty minutes, booked in his calendar like classes he cannot skip.
- `00:57` A modest plan you keep beats an ambitious one you imagine.
- `01:03` Last, a signal. Every Sunday evening, Dimas checks one number: sessions kept this week.
- `01:10` Three of three, carry on. One of three, make the plan smaller, not the guilt bigger.
- `01:18` Put together, a working intention has four parts.
- `01:22` A result you can count.
- `01:26` A date.
- `01:29` A weekly time budget you can defend.
- `01:33` And one signal you check every Sunday.
- `01:37` Intentions with a number and a date get finished.
- `01:42` Intentions with adjectives get abandoned.
- `01:45` Defend a modest time budget.
- `01:48` Measure progress every week, or it quietly stops.
- `01:53` Now it is your turn. Write your one sentence for The Map: a result, a date, a weekly budget and a Sunday signal.
