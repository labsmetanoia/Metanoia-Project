# Not a verdict: the two beliefs under every skill

Film `map-1-3-not-a-verdict` · The Map 1.3

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives: turning fixed self-talk into method language, separating the concern zone from the control zone, and honest self-assessment. The ideas are long-established findings on beliefs about ability and on the limits of personal control, taught here in the lesson's own words. The story and the character are invented. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (119 s)

- `00:04` Nadia's first presentation at her internship goes badly. She loses her place twice.
- `00:11` That night, one sentence plays on repeat: I am just bad at presenting.
- `00:17` It sounds like a fact. It is actually a full stop. Nothing comes after it.
- `00:24` A verdict about yourself leaves nothing to do next.
- `00:29` So she crosses it out.
- `00:33` I have not trained presenting with a method yet. Now there is a next step: rehearse twice, out loud, with a timer.
- `00:42` The second belief is about energy. Nadia worries about the job market, the other interns, and what her supervisor thinks of her.
- `00:51` None of that moves when she pushes. Her rehearsals do. So do the questions she asks.
- `00:58` Energy spent inside your control compounds. Energy spent on the rest evaporates.
- `01:04` So she rehearses twice with a teammate and a timer, and asks him one question: where did you lose me?
- `01:13` Her second presentation is not perfect. It is clearly better. Ability turned out to be a variable.
- `01:21` Your personal audit comes next. It only works at one setting: honest.
- `01:28` That means no self-punishment and no self-promotion. Kind to the person, ruthless about the data.
- `01:35` “I am bad at X” is a full stop.
- `01:40` “I have not trained X with a method yet” is a to-do.
- `01:46` Now it is your turn. Catch one verdict in your own self-talk today, and rewrite it as a method.
