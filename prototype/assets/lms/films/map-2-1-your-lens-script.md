## English (73 s)

- `00:00` Your lens: why copied habits fade.
- `00:03` It is two in the morning. Bima is finishing a report that is due at nine. Again.
- `00:09` He has tried the fixes: a planner, an app, an earlier alarm. Each one lasted about a week.
- `00:17` The fixes fail because they are practices. Think of three layers: practices are what you do, paradigms are the lens you see work through, and principles stay true whether you believe them or not.
- `00:26` So he audits the lens. What would I have to believe for all-night work to make sense? Only pressure makes me work.
- `00:32` Then he tests that belief against evidence. His two best pieces of work this year were both started early.
- `00:38` A new lens: a start I schedule beats a deadline I fear. The practices follow by themselves.
- `00:46` Growth at work follows an order. You start directed. Then self-directed: you own your responses, your direction and your schedule. Only then are you trusted with others.
- `00:55` Three weeks later. It is Monday afternoon, the report is due on Thursday, and it is already half done. He did not find a better app. He changed the lens.
- `01:01` Practices copied without their lens fade in weeks. A changed lens changes many practices at once.
- `01:08` Now it is your turn. Take your least effective habit and ask: what would I have to believe for this to make sense?
