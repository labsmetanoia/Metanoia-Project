# Your lens: why copied habits fade

Film `map-2-1-your-lens` · The Map 2.1

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives and reading: the three layers of principle, paradigm and practice, auditing one paradigm, and the order from directed to self-directed to trusted with others. The ideas are long-established and taught here in the lesson's own words. The story and the character are invented. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (139 s)

- `00:04` It is two in the morning. Bima is finishing a report that is due at nine. Again.
- `00:12` He has tried the fixes: a planner, an app, an earlier alarm. Each one lasted about a week.
- `00:21` The fixes fail because they are practices. The problem sits one layer down.
- `00:27` Think of three layers.
- `00:30` Practices are what you do: the planner, the alarm.
- `00:35` Paradigms are the lens you see work through. His says: only pressure makes me work.
- `00:42` Principles stay true whether he believes them or not. Skill follows steady practice, and tired work is worse work.
- `00:51` So he audits the lens. What would I have to believe for all-night work to make sense?
- `00:59` Then he tests that belief against evidence. His two best pieces of work this year were both started early.
- `01:08` A new lens. A start I schedule beats a deadline I fear. The practices follow by themselves.
- `01:16` One more idea before the next lessons. Growth at work follows an order.
- `01:23` You start directed. Teachers, parents and luck decide the outcome.
- `01:28` Then self-directed. You own your responses, your direction and your schedule.
- `01:34` Only then are you trusted with others. The middle step cannot be skipped.
- `01:40` Three weeks later. It is Monday afternoon, the report is due on Thursday, and it is already half done.
- `01:49` He did not find a better app. He changed the lens, and many practices changed at once.
- `01:57` Practices copied without their lens fade in weeks.
- `02:02` A changed lens changes many practices at once.
- `02:06` Now it is your turn. Take your least effective habit and ask: what would I have to believe for this to make sense?
