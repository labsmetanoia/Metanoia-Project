# The gap: acting inside your control

Film `map-2-2-the-gap` · The Map 2.2

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives and reading: the space between stimulus and response, reactive and ownership language, and initiative that needs nobody's permission. The ideas are long-established and taught here in the lesson's own words. The story and the character are invented. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (123 s)

- `00:04` Ten at night. A message from a teammate: I have not started my part. The presentation is tomorrow.
- `00:13` Sinta's thumbs are already typing something sharp.
- `00:17` But between the message and her reply there is a gap. It is about two seconds wide.
- `00:25` The stimulus is fixed. She did not choose it.
- `00:30` The gap is where she chooses.
- `00:33` And the response is entirely hers.
- `00:37` Ownership is not hustle or aggression. It is refusing to let the stimulus write your reply.
- `00:45` Her first draft: you always make me do everything. That sentence hands the pen to him.
- `00:52` She deletes it.
- `00:55` The second draft begins with I choose. She finishes slide four tonight, and asks for the last two by eight.
- `01:04` Your language is a live diagnostic. I have to, they made me, there is nothing I can do: these are reactive tells.
- `01:13` I choose, I will, here is what I can do: these are ownership tells.
- `01:20` Listen to yourself for one day, and count.
- `01:25` The next morning she does one thing nobody asked for: a one-page checklist for the group's next project.
- `01:33` Initiative inside your control needs no permission. That is exactly why interviewers ask for examples of it.
- `01:41` The stimulus is fixed. The response is yours.
- `01:46` Your words show who holds the pen.
- `01:50` Now it is your turn. Pick one frustrating moment from this week. Find the gap, and rewrite your reply.
