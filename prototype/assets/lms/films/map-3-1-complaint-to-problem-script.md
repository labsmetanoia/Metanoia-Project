# From complaint to problem: the four-step method

Film `map-3-1-complaint-to-problem` · The Map 3.1

**Source note.** Written for this lesson from its own objectives: the four steps, the habit of skipping one of them, a logic tree with no gaps and no overlaps, and a problem written as a gap. The methods are standard problem-solving practice, taught here in the lesson's own words. The case, the numbers and the character are invented for this film and appear in no other course or book. No third-party script, footage, picture or sound.

## English (136 s)

- `00:04` Week nine. Kirana runs her faculty's peer tutoring, and the room is nearly empty.
- `00:11` Her team says: nobody comes any more. That is a complaint. It is not yet a problem she can work on.
- `00:20` Problem solving is a loop with four steps, walked in order.
- `00:26` Understand the problem.
- `00:29` Find the root cause.
- `00:32` Make a plan.
- `00:35` Execute, and adjust.
- `00:38` Most people skip a step, and always the same one. Some stop at the complaint.
- `00:45` Some judge every plan and never make one.
- `00:49` Some have ideas all day, and no dates.
- `00:54` And some act at once, without a diagnosis. Fast, busy, and often wrong.
- `01:00` So Kirana starts at step one, and narrows the complaint with a logic tree.
- `01:07` Who stopped coming?
- `01:10` Since when?
- `01:13` At which stage do we lose them?
- `01:17` The attendance sheets answer: first-years, after midterms, who sign up and then do not come.
- `01:25` A good tree has no gaps and no overlaps: every student sits on exactly one branch.
- `01:32` Then she writes the problem as a gap. Current state: nine first-years a week. Desired state: thirty by the end of term.
- `01:41` And the obstacle: since midterms, the sessions clash with the new lab schedule.
- `01:48` Now it is a problem someone can work on.
- `01:53` A complaint becomes a problem when it has a number and an obstacle.
- `01:59` Notice which step you skip.
- `02:03` Now it is your turn. Take one complaint you made this week and write it as a gap: current, desired, obstacle.
