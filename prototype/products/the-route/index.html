Course Description

The Route is the career acceleration system of the Metanoia platform. After mapping your direction (The Map), building your application arsenal (The Pack), and winning the room (The Rope), you now face the challenge that most early professionals are completely unprepared for: building a career that actually goes somewhere.

Most people are good at their jobs. Very few are deliberate about their careers. The difference is not talent — it is strategy. This course gives you the frameworks, tools, and mindset systems to move from good employee to deliberate professional building toward something specific.

Nine modules take you through the complete early-career growth system: designing your 3–5 year trajectory, learning what high performance actually looks like from a promotion committee's perspective, navigating the political and relational landscape of your organisation, building your reputation inside and outside your company, mastering the timing and execution of the promotion conversation, building mentors and sponsors who advocate for you, knowing when and how to make your next career move, establishing the financial foundations that compound from your first real salary, and developing the leadership presence that earns authority before the title ever arrives.

The summit is a waypoint, not a destination. This is how you plan the full route.

Module 01

Career Architecture

Most early professionals plan their careers by accident: waiting for performance reviews, hoping the right manager notices them, and reacting to opportunities rather than creating them. Career architecture is the discipline of building deliberately. This module teaches you how to design a 3–5 year professional trajectory with specific milestones, realistic role progressions, and clear decision gates — so that every year you spend in a role is taking you somewhere specific, not just keeping you busy.

4 lessons ~75 min Available
02
Module 02

The Performance Game

Working hard is not the same as performing in the way that promotion committees actually notice and reward. This module unpacks what high performance really means from the perspective of the people who evaluate it: how to understand what your organisation's performance framework actually measures, how to make your contributions visible without appearing self-promotional, how to seek and act on feedback in ways that accelerate development, and how to maintain elite performance habits without burning out.

4 lessons ~80 min Locked
03
Module 03

Managing Up, Across, and Down

Professional success is not just about what you deliver — it is about how well you navigate the human system around what you deliver. This module covers the three directions of organisational influence: managing up (understanding your manager's priorities, communicating in formats they value, and making their life easier); managing across (building productive peer relationships and cross-functional credibility); and managing down (leading without formal authority, building influence before the title).

4 lessons ~80 min Locked
04
Module 04

Building Professional Visibility

The professionals who advance fastest are not always the most talented — they are often the most visible. Professional visibility is a skill, not a personality trait, and it operates on two tracks: your internal reputation (who knows your name and what it means within your organisation) and your external presence (your professional profile, thought leadership, and network beyond your company walls). This module builds both, systematically and without performance.

4 lessons ~75 min Locked
05
Module 05

The Promotion Game

Promotions are not given — they are earned through a combination of demonstrated performance, strategic timing, and a well-executed conversation. This module teaches you how promotion decisions are actually made (the processes most employees never see), how to build the evidence base for your advancement over months rather than days, how to time the promotion conversation correctly, and how to have it in a way that positions you for a yes even when the timing is imperfect.

4 lessons ~75 min Locked
06
Module 06

Mentors, Sponsors, and Your Inner Circle

The most powerful accelerant in any professional career is a strong network of relationships that actively invest in your success. But most professionals confuse mentors with sponsors — and only pursue one when they need both. This module defines the distinction (mentors advise; sponsors advocate), teaches you how to build and maintain each type of relationship with integrity, and helps you design your "inner circle" — the four or five people whose connection to your career will matter most in the next five years.

4 lessons ~70 min Locked
07
Module 07

The Second Move

The second career move is among the most consequential decisions an early professional makes — and the most commonly mistimed. Leave too early and you haven't built enough career capital. Stay too long and you've plateaued. Move to the wrong role and you've erased momentum. This module gives you the frameworks to evaluate when you are ready to move, how to assess your next role against your career architecture, and how to execute the transition — internally or externally — without burning the relationships and reputation you have built. For application mechanics, your toolkit is The Pack; for interview performance, The Rope.

4 lessons ~80 min Locked
08
Module 08

Early Career Financial Intelligence

Your first real professional salary is the first moment financial decisions meaningfully compound. Most early professionals spend the year learning their role and default into financial habits — savings rates, spending patterns, investment inaction — that are extremely difficult to change later. This module covers the financial foundations that matter in the first five years of professional life: understanding your total compensation and how to benchmark it, building a savings discipline that works on any income, an introduction to investment vehicles available in your market, and the financial planning horizon that converts a first salary into long-term financial independence.

4 lessons ~70 min Locked
09
Module 09

Leadership Foundations

Leadership is not a title — it is a pattern of behavior that earns influence from others regardless of formal authority. This final module focuses on the leadership foundations that early professionals must develop before the managerial title arrives: executive communication presence, the discipline of developing others, building a reputation as someone worth following, and operating with the strategic mindset of a leader even while functioning as an individual contributor. The professional who arrives at their first management role already thinking like a leader is the one who makes the transition successfully.

4 lessons ~80 min Locked

Module 01 · Career Architecture Available

Career Trajectory Blueprint

Assignment Brief

Using the frameworks from Module 1, design your personal Career Trajectory Blueprint. Part A — Career Capital Inventory: complete the four-dimension career capital inventory from Lesson 1.4; rate yourself honestly on skills, knowledge, relationships, and reputation; identify your three strongest assets and your two most significant gaps. Part B — 3–5 Year Trajectory Design: map your current role → bridge role → target role; for each stage, specify the role title or type, the organisation profile, the career capital you need to build, and the approximate timeframe. Part C — Decision Gates and Milestones: set three concrete milestones for the next 18 months with specific, observable criteria; define at least one decision gate and one career red line.

Format: Written document (700–900 words) + visual trajectory map 100 pts Self-paced
Module 02 · The Performance Game Locked — complete Module 2 first

Performance Audit + Visibility Plan

Assignment Brief

Part A — Performance Framework Audit: identify and document your organisation's formal performance framework (review criteria, competencies, KPIs); for each criterion, rate your current performance honestly (1–5 scale) and identify the one specific behavior change that would move your rating one point higher. Part B — Invisible Criteria Map: identify 3 informal performance criteria in your organisation that are not written down but clearly influence career outcomes, and document how you will demonstrate each in the next quarter. Part C — Feedback Sourcing Plan: identify three people who can give you the most valuable developmental feedback right now; for each, write the specific question you will ask and the meeting format you will use.

Format: Three-part written document (600–800 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 03 · Managing Up, Across, and Down Locked — complete Module 3 first

Organisational Navigation Map

Assignment Brief

Part A — Manager Profile: document what you currently know about your manager's real priorities, decision-making style, preferred communication formats, and professional pressures; identify one specific behavior change that would make you more valuable to them this quarter. Part B — Peer Relationship Inventory: list your five most important cross-functional relationships; for each, note the current state (strong / developing / weak), why this person matters to your trajectory, and one specific action to strengthen the relationship in the next 60 days. Part C — Influence Without Authority: describe one situation where you successfully influenced an outcome without formal authority — what you did, and what you would do differently now.

Format: Three-part document (600–800 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 04 · Professional Visibility Locked — complete Module 4 first

Personal Visibility System Design

Assignment Brief

Design your personal visibility system with two tracks. Track A — Internal: identify your current internal reputation ("if my name came up in a senior meeting today, what would they say about me?"); define the reputation you want in 12 months; list three specific internal actions — presentations, project leadership, cross-functional contributions — you will take this quarter to move from current to target reputation. Track B — External: assess your current LinkedIn presence; choose one external visibility format to begin (article series, industry community contribution, speaker application, mentoring program); write your content or contribution plan for the next 90 days: topics, cadence, and success criteria.

Format: Two-track visibility plan (600–800 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 05 · The Promotion Game Locked — complete Module 5 first

Promotion Case File

Assignment Brief

Build your personal Promotion Case File — a private document you will maintain and update over time, designed to be ready the moment you initiate the promotion conversation. Section 1 — The Target Level: describe the role and responsibilities at the next level above yours — the skills, behaviors, and contributions expected. Section 2 — Your Evidence Base: for each next-level criterion, document specific, quantified examples of where you have already demonstrated it; identify the gaps. Section 3 — Gap Closing Plan: for each evidence gap, define the specific action you will take in the next 90 days to generate evidence. Section 4 — Timing Assessment: based on your organisation's review cycle and your evidence base, identify the earliest credible window for your promotion conversation and the triggers that would adjust the timing.

Format: Four-section document (700–1,000 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 06 · Mentors, Sponsors & Inner Circle Locked — complete Module 6 first

Inner Circle Blueprint

Assignment Brief

Design your professional Inner Circle. Part A — Current State Audit: map your current significant professional relationships into three categories — mentors (active), potential sponsors (people who know your work and could advocate for you), and peers (colleagues who keep you honest and accountable); be honest about which relationships are genuine and which are cordial but shallow. Part B — Gaps and Targets: identify the two most important relationship gaps in your Inner Circle; for each, name one specific person (or type of person) who could fill it, and write your outreach approach — the value exchange you are proposing and the initial message you will send. Part C — Maintenance System: design your relationship maintenance calendar for the next six months — who you will check in with, at what cadence, and in what format.

Format: Three-part document with relationship map (600–800 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 07 · The Second Move Locked — complete Module 7 first

Career Move Evaluation Framework

Assignment Brief

Apply the second-move framework to your specific situation. Part A — Readiness Assessment: using the career capital framework from Module 7, assess your current readiness to make a career move — what capital you have built, what is not yet built, and the cost of moving now versus waiting 12 months. Part B — Opportunity Evaluation: identify one specific next role (internal or external) that aligns with your career architecture from Assignment 1, and evaluate it against your trajectory framework: does it build the right capital, open the next door, and does the organisation, manager, and team accelerate or stall your growth? Part C — Transition Plan: if you were to make this move in the next 6 months, outline the execution — how you would manage the conversation with your current manager and ensure a clean, reputation-preserving transition.

Format: Three-part document (700–900 words) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 08 · Early Career Finance Locked — complete Module 8 first

Financial Foundation Plan

Assignment Brief

Build your personal financial foundation plan. Part A — Total Compensation Audit: calculate your true annual total compensation package (base + all allowances + THR + insurance + bonus estimate); benchmark it against market data and quantify the gap in real terms. Part B — Savings Architecture: design your savings structure using the Module 8 framework — emergency fund target and current status, short-term goal fund, and long-term investment capital — with specific monthly amounts for each bucket. Part C — Investment Orientation: describe your investment starting point — what you know, what you don't, and your initial plan for where long-term investment capital will go. Disclaimer: this assignment is an educational planning exercise; consult a licensed financial advisor before making real investment decisions.

Format: Three-part financial plan (600–800 words + tables) 100 pts Self-paced
Module 09 · Leadership Foundations Locked — complete Module 9 first

Leadership Presence Portfolio

Assignment Brief

Compile your Leadership Presence Portfolio — a record of the leadership behaviors you have practiced as an individual contributor. Part A — Communication Audit: record a 3–5 minute video of yourself briefing an imaginary senior leader on a work project or challenge, applying bottom-line-up-front structure, clear language, and composed delivery; submit the video and a 150-word self-assessment on your executive presence. Part B — Development Contribution: document one instance where you helped a peer develop — a feedback conversation, a knowledge transfer, a coaching moment — its impact, and what you learned about developing others. Part C — Leadership Mindset Reflection: write a 300-word reflection on the shift from individual-contributor thinking to leader thinking — where you have already made this shift, where you are still operating from the old frame, and what you will change.

Format: Video (3–5 min) + self-assessment + written parts B and C 100 pts Self-paced
